The Blurred Boundary

Care, Protection and Control

Location & Year
Personal practice · 2026
Medium
Still Life Photography, Portrait Photography, Collage
Type
Photographic Narrative
Scope
34 Pages · Desk Archive, The Measured Body

Design Statement

This project explores the blurred boundary between care, protection, and control within a mother-daughter relationship. It began with an old school schedule written by my mother, which made me realise my growth had been arranged and measured from an early age. Through still life photography of desks, uniforms, and measured bodies, I ask: when a daughter is shaped into a perfect student, what part of herself is left unmeasured?

SECTION 01 INSPIRATION & STATEMENT

During the summer, I accidentally found an old school schedule written by my mother.

It made me realise that my growth had been arranged and measured from an early age. Textbooks, notes, exam papers, and stationery become archives of a planned life, showing how academic achievement gradually occupied my daily space. I then constructed the image of the “perfect student” through school uniform, controlled posture, and staged portraiture.

SECTION 02 CALLIGRAPHY APRON & INK TRACES

Calligraphy Apron on Hanger: I practiced calligraphy with a brush pen at the beginning of primary school. The ink spots permanently etched into the cotton apron record countless quiet afternoons spent sitting straight and copying ancient masters.

Laid-out Apron & Nine-Grid Practice Sheets: The laid-out apron reveals the organic accumulation of splashed ink, contrasted with the rigid nine-grid practice sheet repeatedly inscribing “Where there is a will, there is a way.”

SECTION 03 PRIMARY SCHOOL & EARLY DISCIPLINE

Primary School Desk Overview: Neatly aligned textbooks, pen holders, timers, and examination supplies constituted the familiar territory of childhood, where every item was positioned according to strict order and efficiency.

Mother’s Reminder Sticker & Exam Guidelines: “Read the questions carefully, write neatly, double check your work, keep it up!”

My mom put this sticker on my pencil case after the first parent-teacher conference. Every time I unzipped it during tests, these instructions were there to remind me of the standards expected.

Ballet Leotard & Satin Pointe Shoes: Ballet leotard and pointe shoes: physical discipline through dance alongside academic demands, training muscle memory and posture to embody perfection.

SECTION 04 STUDY MATERIALS & TRANSITION

Senior Primary Study Desk & Stationery: Textbooks grew thicker, and correction tapes, highlighters, and draft pads multiplied across the desk as learning transitioned rapidly toward rigorous examination prep.

Heart-Cutout Pink Pencil Case & Pens: The pastel pink pencil case with a delicate heart cutout juxtaposed against severe test papers, revealing quiet childhood warmth within structured routines.

Loose-Leaf Error Notebook & Reinforcement Rings: The loose-leaf binder allowed flexible restructuring of missed exam problems, each punched hole reinforced to withstand endless cycles of revision.

SECTION 05 EXAM DRILLS & METHODICAL REPETITION

Middle School Entrance Drills & Grammar Workbooks: These are the practice books and notebooks from when I was preparing for the middle school entrance exams. We were required to complete English grammar workbooks three times through until precision became instinct.

Grammar Notes Spread & Error Analysis: Black ink recorded theoretical grammar rules, while red ink dissected exceptions and recurring pitfalls. Notes were maintained with architectural precision.

6th Grade Writing Outline & Multi-color Pen: I wrote this in 6th grade as an outline for a writing competition. Introduction, transitions, rhetorical emphasis, and emotional resolution were all engineered like a structural diagram.

Los Angeles High School Application Desk: This image represents the period when I was applying to a high school in Los Angeles. School brochures, TOEFL vocabulary, and essay drafts merged as time zones shifted toward California.

Campus Guides & Standardized Testing Pens: Campus maps and academic guides laid alongside rows of vibrant highlighting markers, aligning global exploration with heightened self-discipline.

SECTION 08 PROJECT INTRODUCTION & MEASURED TORSO

Project Introduction

This project explores the blurred boundary between care, protection, and control within high-expectation family environments. It began with an old school schedule written by my mother, which made me realize that my growth had been meticulously arranged and measured from an early age. This personal discovery led me to reflect on a wider experience shared by many students: between classes, exams, tutoring, and deadlines, we are trained to become the “good student” expected by family, school, and society.

Through still life photography, I photographed desks from different stages of my education. Textbooks, notes, exam papers, and stationery become archives of a planned life, showing how academic achievement gradually occupied my daily space. I then constructed the image of the “perfect student” through school uniform, controlled posture, and staged portraiture. This figure appears calm and successful, but also artificial, as if she has been shaped into an ideal role.

“In the final images, the body enters darkness. Hands tighten the collar, adjust the tie, and measure the body with a tape measure. Measurement becomes a symbol of evaluation and standardization, suggesting how external expectations move from the desk onto the body itself.”

This project does not aim to reject my mother’s love, but to reveal the complexity within care. A carefully planned life may offer safety, but it can also reduce the space for self-discovery. By photographing desks, uniforms, and measured bodies, I want to ask: when a daughter is shaped into a perfect student, what part of herself is left unmeasured?
