Sierra Canyon: Everyday Frictions & Spatial Diagnosis

A time-based behavioral mapping and topographical investigation of Sierra Canyon School on the edge of the Santa Susana Canyon, uncovering the structural tensions between natural grade, freeway infrastructure, and student flow.

Research Methodology & Diagnostic Statement

Perched along the rugged topographic threshold of the Santa Susana Canyon and bounded by the Ronald Reagan Freeway in Chatsworth, Los Angeles, Sierra Canyon School embodies acute spatial contradictions between natural mountain grade and rapid vehicular transit.

Through extensive on-site mapping, temporal behavioral tracking, and axonometric dissection, this research diagnoses critical everyday frictions across campus: acute vehicle-pedestrian bottlenecks during morning drop-off, abrupt and disorienting grade transitions connected by purely utilitarian staircases, and a lack of climate-tempered outdoor gathering nodes. By decoding student, faculty, and vehicular flows into empirical spatial data, this research uncovers the underlying structural tensions, establishing an indispensable evidentiary baseline for master planning and tectonic intervention.
